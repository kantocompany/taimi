Key observations

  • OpenAI Codex Go ($8/mo, platformP) is the cheapest paid subscription; GitHub Copilot Pro ($10/mo) is the cheapest standalone individual plan. Mistral Vibe ($14.99/mo) is the only EU-headquartered vendor tracked here — critical for data sovereignty.
  • Team seat costs range ~6.6× from $19 (Copilot Business) to $125 (Claude Code Premium Seat, monthly billing); Kiro dropped its team tiers entirely (absent from its pricing page as of 2026-08-30), leaving Enterprise as its only multi-seat option.
  • Usage-based is apples to oranges: Copilot moved to AI Credits (token-based, $0.01/credit) June 2026 — previously per request ($0.04). Anthropic/OpenAI/Mistral charge per token, Replit per effort-based Agent task; Devin publishes no per-unit rate. Cursor bills on-demand usage in dollars, in arrears, once the included pool is spent (Pro+/Ultra buy 3×/20× larger pools).
  • Among token rates tracked here, Claude Haiku 4.5 output ($5/M) is the cheapest — 10× less than Claude Fable 5.1 output ($50/M). Claude Sonnet 5 ($10/M output) sits near the low end — the scheduled 2026-09-01 increase to $15/M was cancelled, making the introductory rate permanent. (Mistral's Devstral 2, previously the cheapest rate shown here at $2/M output, was retired by the vendor on 2026-07-31.)
  • Platform bundlingP: Claude Code, Codex, Vibe, and Devin subscription plans include their full platforms (Claude.ai, ChatGPT, Le Chat, and — since Cognition folded Windsurf into Devin Desktop in June 2026 — the Devin Desktop IDE). Their prices aren't directly comparable to standalone tools like Cursor or Kiro. Google Antigravity (agent-first IDE, Free–$100/mo tracked) replaced Gemini Code Assist for individual developers at Google I/O 2026.
  • Self-hosted / on-prem: Mistral (EU data, fine-tune), Cline (open-source BYOK). Kiro (AWS) is the only AWS-native tool — spec-driven IDE with hooks and steering files; Pro at $20/user. Replaces Amazon Q Developer (IDE plugins and subscriptions EoS April 2027).
  • 2026 shift: subscription overage increasingly runs through credit systems. GitHub Copilot replaced premium requests with token-metered AI Credits (June; $0.01/credit, billed in arrears against a budget); Kiro sells prepaid credit packs (July; $0.04/credit); Google Antigravity meters overage in purchasable credits; Claude's opt-in usage credits are prepaid and bill at standard API token rates. The per-credit price is visible — the number of credits a given task will consume is rarely predictable in advance. Two tracked vendors run the other way in plain dollars: Cursor (in arrears) and Augment, which dropped credits for list-price tokens plus a disclosed flat 40% service fee. Purchased top-ups commonly expire 12 months after purchase (Kiro, Augment).
  • No SWE-bench scores here, on purpose. Vendor publications are self-reported with cherry-picked configurations, the official leaderboard doesn't cover consumer agent products (it ranks model+scaffold combos), and variants (Verified, Pro, Lite) aren't comparable. Showing a single number next to "—" would imply a ranking the data can't support.
